  - type: "text"
    body: |
      Under the law, health care providers need to give clients who don't have insurance, or who aren't using their insurance, an estimate of the expected cost of care before that care begins. This page explains that right and lays out what a Good Faith Estimate looks like for therapy with me.
  - type: "list"
    heading: "Your rights under the No Surprises Act"
    items:
      - "You have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate explaining how much your care is expected to cost."
      - "You can ask me for a Good Faith Estimate before you schedule a service, or at any point during care."
      - "If you're billed significantly more than this estimate (generally $400 or more above it), you have the right to dispute the bill."
      - "Keep a copy of your estimate. For questions or more information about your right to a Good Faith Estimate, visit [www.cms.gov/nosurprises](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises) or call 1-800-985-3059."
  - type: "text"
    heading: "What determines your estimate"
    body: |
      I am an out-of-network, self-pay provider — I don't bill insurance directly. Your estimate depends on the type of session, how often we meet, and how long care is expected to last, which we'll talk through together before your first full session. The figures below are my standard fee schedule as of the date at the top of this page.
  - type: "list"
    heading: "Current fees"
    items:
      - "Individual session, 50 minutes — $180"
      - "Extended individual sessions, up to 40 extra min — $20 per additional 10 minutes"
      - "Sessions with multiple individuals (couples or family), 80 minutes — $230"
      - "Initial consultation, 20 minutes — free"
      - "Discounted, sliding-scale rate — ask me directly; a limited number of spots are available"
  - type: "text"
    heading: "How long care is expected to last"
    body: |
      This varies a great deal by person, so I can't give a single number up front. After the initial consultation and the assessment period (the first 1–2 sessions), I'll give you my honest sense of frequency and likely length of care based on what we're working on, and we'll revisit that estimate together as things progress or change.
  - type: "text"
    heading: "Questions or a bill that doesn't match your estimate"
    body: |
      If something you're billed comes in substantially higher than the estimate you were given, you have the right to dispute it — contact me directly first at (425) 780-6524, or use the federal patient-provider dispute resolution process described at [www.cms.gov/nosurprises](https://www.cms.gov/nosurprises). You can also request an updated written estimate from me at any time.
