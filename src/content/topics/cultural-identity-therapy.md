      If you grew up translating — not just language, but expectations, between your parents' world and the one you actually live in — you know how heavy that job can get. I'm Samar Farange, a licensed therapist based in Seattle, offering online therapy across Washington state to people navigating identity and family expectations between two cultures, in English or Farsi.

      This comes up a lot with people who are the first in their family born or raised here, or who moved as kids and grew up straddling two very different sets of rules for who they're allowed to be.

  - type: "text"
    heading: "What sessions look like"
    body: |
      We untangle two things that tend to get fused together: honoring someone, and organizing your whole life around their comfort. Those aren't the same, even though it rarely feels that way growing up. A lot of this work is figuring out which decisions are actually yours to make, and building the ability to disappoint someone in one choice without it meaning you've betrayed the relationship. I wrote more about this in [Honoring Your Parents and Disappointing Them](../writing/honoring-your-parents-and-disappointing-them.html).
