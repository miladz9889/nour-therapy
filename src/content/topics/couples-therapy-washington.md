      Relationship trouble doesn't need both people in the room to start working on it. I'm Samar Farange, a licensed therapist, and I work with individuals across Washington state navigating hurdles within their relationship. This can include communication that's broken down, resentment that's built up quietly, rebuilding trust after something broke it, or just the sense of having drifted from partners into roommates.

      Most people who reach out are dealing with something specific such as a recurring fight that never actually resolves, a betrayal that needs to be worked through, or a slow drift that's hard to name but easy to feel.
  - type: list
    heading: Who this is for
    items:
      - Arguments that circle back to the same unresolved thing, over and over
      - Rebuilding trust after a betrayal or a broken agreement
      - Feeling like co-managers of a shared life rather than partners — see [The Quiet Shift From Partners to Roommates](../writing/the-quiet-shift-from-partners-to-roommates.html)
      - Deciding what comes next, how to repair, how to move forward, or whether to stay
      - Resentment that's accumulated over a hard season and hasn't been talked through
      - Wanting to work on the relationship when your partner isn't ready to join in yet
  - type: text
    heading: What sessions look like
    body: A lot of this work happens with just one partner, especially at the start. You don't need your partner's buy-in to begin untangling what's going on, and real movement in one person often shifts the dynamic between you. Your partner is welcome to join for a few sessions, when it's useful, where a full 80 minutes is provided rather than the standard 50. Details are on the [fees & insurance](../index.html#fees) page.
