      Some conflict doesn't belong to one person — it lives in the patterns between people: the roles everyone's settled into, the loyalties nobody questions out loud, the thing the whole family knows but nobody names. I'm Samar Farange, a licensed therapist, and I work with individuals and families across Washington state untangling exactly that kind of conflict, in English or Farsi.

      This often shows up as one person being cast as "the problem," old roles from childhood that never got updated, or boundaries that keep getting tested no matter how clearly they're stated.

      Like relationship work, most of this happens with one person — you don't need the rest of your family on board to start changing your part of a long-standing pattern. Family members are welcome to join for a session when it's useful, and joint sessions are available for families who want to work on something together from the start — those run 80 minutes rather than the standard 50. Details are on the [fees & insurance](../index.html#fees) page.
