Most relationships don't break over one big thing. They drift, quietly, during whatever stretch of life happens to be the hardest. That could be a new job, a loss, an illness, a new baby, a move, money stress that won't let up, the list goes on. Nobody decides to stop being partners. It just gets harder to find the same time and energy you once had when seeking out or beginning the relationship. Partnership requires constant effort, care and attention, and if not prioritized, something else slowly takes its place.

That something is usually logistics. You still talk about the schedule, the bills, who's picking up what, but less and less about what's underneath all that. The desire, connection, mystery, excitement, and fun. Even if neither of you say it out loud, the thoughts of who did more, who sacrificed more, who gets to be tired start creeping in. Whoever is carrying the invisible mental load starts to feel less like a teammate and more like a manager with an employee who doesn't show up. Resentment builds in the gaps nobody has time to talk through. Affection and real conversation, the kind that used to come easily, start running on a deficit, because there's nothing left at the end of the day to offer each other.

None of that means the relationship has gone awry. It means two people are under real pressure, and the ordinary friction of that is showing up as distance, short tempers, or a  feeling of being on separate teams when you used to be on the same one. Most couples move through a season like this at some point. The relationship isn't broken; it's just needing some intention.

At the same time, there's a difference between ordinary strain and a pattern that's settled in. Whether its the same fight circling back with no resolution or one of you feeling consistently unseen in what you carry,  those patterns don't mean it's too late. They mean the relationship needs more attention than it's  getting, before the distance becomes the default.

If you're not sure where your relationship stands and what page the two of you are on, that uncertainty in itself is reason to talk to someone. You don't need a reason to justify starting, and you don't need to arrive with an agreed-upon diagnosis of what's "wrong." That is exactly what we will explore in our first conversation.
